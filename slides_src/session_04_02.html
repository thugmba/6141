MODULE 41-2. Purpose and Outcomes
Session 4.2: AI Applications in Business Decision-Making Part 1: Predictive Customer Analytics and Churn
THE BUSINESS SITUATION
  • A subscription telecom company loses customers every month to churn (customers cancelling their contract)
  • Its retention team can call customers or assign them an account manager, but every contact costs money, and calling customers who would stay anyway wastes it
  • The head of customer retention asks: which of our current customers should we contact first, and will the campaign pay for itself? Today, you turn churn predictions into revenue at risk and a campaign plan with a measurable return

Today's flow: 60 min lecture, then 120 min hands-on lab

EXPECTED OUTCOMES

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  1. "Who is likely to leave?" Train a churn model on past customers and score current customers with a churn probability
  2. "How much money is at stake?" Calculate revenue at risk (churn probability x yearly customer value) and rank customers by it
  3. "Will the campaign pay off?" Calculate a campaign's expected net benefit and ROI (Return on Investment), choose who to target, and fix a ValueError caused by missing feature columns

MODULE 43. Business Context
Pricing a Retention Campaign
KEY POINTS
  • Four ideas, one example (current customer ACT-101):
  • CLV (Customer Lifetime Value) is the total value of a customer over the whole relationship, often several years.
  • A campaign is worth it for a customer when expected saved revenue is larger than the contact cost.
  • Net benefit vs ROI: net benefit = saved revenue - cost, in dollars; ROI = net benefit / cost x 100, in percent.

MODULE 46. Keywords
6 Terms You Need Today
TERM 1

Churn

What: customers ending their relationship with the company

Why: in subscription businesses, keeping a customer is usually cheaper than winning a new one

TERM 2

Churn Probability

What: the model's estimate, from 0 to 1, that a customer will leave

Why: ranks customers by risk instead of treating everyone the same

TERM 3

Revenue at Risk

What: churn probability x annual value

Why: turns a probability into dollars a manager can budget against

TERM 4

Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)

What: the total value of a customer over the whole relationship

Why: decides how much a customer is worth saving

TERM 5

Net Benefit and ROI

What: saved revenue minus cost (dollars), and net benefit divided by cost (percent)

Why: the two numbers a finance team asks for before approving a campaign

TERM 6

Targeting Threshold

What: the minimum churn probability for a customer to be contacted

Why: sets the size and cost of the campaign

MODULE 49. Code Demonstration 1
Learn from the Past, Score the Present
 YourName_S42_demo_1.pyInput → Process → Output
# 1. Input: past customers (with outcomes) and current customers
import pandas as pd
from sklearn.pipeline import Pipeline
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler
from sklearn.linear_model import LogisticRegression
history = pd.read_csv("data/session_04_02_dataset.csv")
active = pd.read_csv("data/session_04_02_active.csv")
features = ["Tenure_Months_Count", "Customer_Support_Calls_Count",
            "Usage_Drop_Last_30_Days_Pct"]

# 2. Process: train on history, then score the active customers
model = Pipeline([
    ("scale", StandardScaler()),
    ("classify", LogisticRegression()),
])
model.fit(history[features], history["Churned_Within_60_Days_Binary"])
active["Churn_Probability"] = model.predict_proba(active[features])[:, 1].round(3)

# 3. Output: the active customers, most at risk first
print(active.sort_values("Churn_Probability", ascending=False)[
    ["Subscriber_ID", "Tenure_Months_Count", "Churn_Probability"]
])
HOW IT WORKS
  • Two files are loaded: history (past customers, with the answer) and active (current customers, without it).
  • The Pipeline scales the 3 features and fits a logistic regression on the history (section 7.1).
  • model.predict_proba(active[features])[:, 1] scores the current customers: the second column is the probability of leaving.
READ THE OUTPUT
  • The top 3 have all been customers for less than 8 months, like the past customers who left.

MODULE 49. Code Demonstration 2
Revenue at Risk
 YourName_S42_demo_2.pyInput → Process → Output
# 1. Input: train and score, as in Micro-Example 1
import pandas as pd
from sklearn.pipeline import Pipeline
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler
from sklearn.linear_model import LogisticRegression
history = pd.read_csv("data/session_04_02_dataset.csv")
active = pd.read_csv("data/session_04_02_active.csv")
features = ["Tenure_Months_Count", "Customer_Support_Calls_Count",
            "Usage_Drop_Last_30_Days_Pct"]
model = Pipeline([("scale", StandardScaler()),
                  ("classify", LogisticRegression())])
model.fit(history[features], history["Churned_Within_60_Days_Binary"])
active["Churn_Probability"] = model.predict_proba(active[features])[:, 1].round(3)

# 2. Process: one year of value, and the part of it at risk
active["Annual_Value_USD"] = (active["Monthly_Subscription_Fee_USD"] * 12).round(2)
active["Revenue_At_Risk_USD"] = (
    active["Churn_Probability"] * active["Annual_Value_USD"]
).round(2)
ranked = active.sort_values("Revenue_At_Risk_USD", ascending=False)

# 3. Output: the ranking and the total
print(ranked[["Subscriber_ID", "Churn_Probability", "Annual_Value_USD",
              "Revenue_At_Risk_USD"]])
print(f"Total revenue at risk: ${active['Revenue_At_Risk_USD'].sum():,.2f}")
HOW IT WORKS
  • Part 1 repeats the training and scoring of Micro-Example 1.
  • Monthly_Subscription_Fee_USD * 12 is one year of revenue from each customer.
  • Multiplying by the churn probability gives the revenue at risk (section 7.2).
READ THE OUTPUT
  • The order differs from Micro-Example 1: ACT-101 moves to the top because it pays the most, and ACT-110 jumps to fourth despite its lower probability.

MODULE 49. Code Demonstration 3
Assign a Campaign to Each Customer
 YourName_S42_demo_3.pyInput → Process → Output
# 1. Input: train and score, as in Micro-Example 2
import pandas as pd
from sklearn.pipeline import Pipeline
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler
from sklearn.linear_model import LogisticRegression
history = pd.read_csv("data/session_04_02_dataset.csv")
active = pd.read_csv("data/session_04_02_active.csv")
features = ["Tenure_Months_Count", "Customer_Support_Calls_Count",
            "Usage_Drop_Last_30_Days_Pct"]
model = Pipeline([("scale", StandardScaler()),
                  ("classify", LogisticRegression())])
model.fit(history[features], history["Churned_Within_60_Days_Binary"])
active["Churn_Probability"] = model.predict_proba(active[features])[:, 1].round(3)
active["Annual_Value_USD"] = (active["Monthly_Subscription_Fee_USD"] * 12).round(2)

# 2. Process: rules from the cheapest to the most valuable campaign
active["Campaign"] = "Newsletter"
active.loc[active["Churn_Probability"] >= 0.4, "Campaign"] = "Retention_Call"
active.loc[
    (active["Churn_Probability"] >= 0.7) & (active["Annual_Value_USD"] >= 1500),
    "Campaign",
] = "VIP_Account_Manager"

# 3. Output: how many customers get each campaign
print(active["Campaign"].value_counts())
print(active[["Subscriber_ID", "Churn_Probability", "Campaign"]])
HOW IT WORKS
  • Part 1 scores the customers and computes their annual value, as before.
  • active["Campaign"] = "Newsletter" gives everyone the default.
  • The first .loc[] line overwrites the default with "Retention_Call" for customers at 0.4 or above.
READ THE OUTPUT
Campaign Newsletter 4 Retention_Call 4 ...
  • 2 customers get a personal account manager, 4 get a call, and 4 only get the newsletter.
