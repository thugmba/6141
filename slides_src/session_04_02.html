Step 2: How Much Profit Is at Risk?
 YourName_S42_step_2.pyInput → Process → Output
# 1. Input: score the current members, as in Step 1
import pandas as pd
from sklearn.linear_model import LogisticRegression
from sklearn.pipeline import make_pipeline
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler
history = pd.read_csv("data/session_04_02_dataset.csv", index_col="Member_ID")
active = pd.read_csv("data/session_04_02_active.csv", index_col="Member_ID")
features = ["Months_As_Member_Count", "Visits_Last_Month_Count",
            "Days_Since_Last_Visit_Count"]
model = make_pipeline(StandardScaler(), LogisticRegression())
model.fit(history[features], history["Stopped_Visiting_Binary"])
active["Chance"] = model.predict_proba(active[features])[:, 1].round(3)

# 2. Process: one year of profit, and the part of it that is at risk
active["Annual_Profit"] = active["Monthly_Profit_TWD"] * 12
active["Profit_At_Risk"] = (active["Chance"] * active["Annual_Profit"]).round(0)

# 3. Output: the members ranked by profit at risk, and the total
ranked = active.sort_values("Profit_At_Risk", ascending=False)
print(ranked[["Chance", "Annual_Profit", "Profit_At_Risk"]])
total = active["Profit_At_Risk"].sum()
print(f"Total profit at risk: NT${total:,.0f}")
HOW IT WORKS
  • Yearly profit = monthly profit x 12
  • Profit at risk = chance x yearly profit: the expected loss

MODULE 4Step 2
Why Multiply by the Chance?
  • A member with a 0.4 chance costs, on average, 40% of their profit, not 100%
  • The expected value is a fair average over many members like them
  • One year of profit is a cautious basis; a member's whole value over many years is the CLV (Customer Lifetime Value)

Step 3: Is a Coupon Worth It for Each Member?

# 1. Input: score the members and add Annual_Profit, as in Step 2
# (full code: section 8 of the reading)

# 2. Process: the expected net value of a free-drink coupon for each member
COUPON_COST = 45        # a free drink costs the shop NT$45
SUCCESS_RATE = 0.25     # a coupon brings back 1 in 4 members who would stop
saved = active["Chance"] * SUCCESS_RATE * active["Annual_Profit"]
active["Coupon_Net"] = (saved - COUPON_COST).round(0)
worth_it = active[active["Coupon_Net"] > 0]

# 3. Output: each member's coupon value, then the campaign
ranked = active.sort_values("Coupon_Net", ascending=False)
print(ranked[["Chance", "Annual_Profit", "Coupon_Net"]])
net = worth_it["Coupon_Net"].sum()
print(f"Coupons worth sending: {len(worth_it)} of {len(active)}")
print(f"Net benefit: NT${net:,.0f}")
HOW IT WORKS
  • Saved = chance x success rate x yearly profit
  • Coupon net = saved - NT$45; send only where it is above 0
